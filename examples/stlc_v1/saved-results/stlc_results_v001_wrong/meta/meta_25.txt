# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

*   **Multi-Stage Redex Injection (The "Power Move")**: The current best program (**Generation 19**, score 12.43) and its successors (**Generations 21 and 22**, score 12.41) utilize a tiered approach to redexes. By explicitly constructing `App(App(Lam(Lam(...))))` structures (Redex-Chains) and allocating 60% of the budget to the inner body, these programs consistently achieve `applied_lambdas_proportions_average` between 0.59 and 0.62. **Generation 22** specifically refined this by adding "Deep Currying," which searches the context for variables that can be applied multiple times to reach a target type.
*   **Context-Inhabited Argument Selection**: A primary driver for high `used_vars_proportions_average` (0.41 in **Generation 19**) is biasing the `arg_ty` of an `App` node toward types already present in the context. **Generation 23** (score 12.35) and **Generation 20** (score 12.33) reinforced this by using a 75% probability to pick an existing context type for arguments, ensuring that variables are not just declared but actively consumed.
*   **Strategic Budget Scaling for Structural Depth**: The best program (**Generation 19**) successfully pushed `tm_sizes_average` to 16.45 by using an initial budget of 110 and a "greedy" budget distribution (60% for lambda bodies, 20% for arguments). This prevents the premature truncation seen in **Generation 16** (size 9.94), allowing for the 0.96 `unique_subterms` score which indicates high structural complexity without repetition.
*   **Tiered Variable Lookup with Callable Priority**: **Generations 21 and 22** implemented a hierarchy: first, direct type matches; second, "callables" (functions in context returning the `target_ty`). This "Medium Priority" logic ensures that complex application structures are grounded in valid context variables, maintaining a 1.00 `good_ratio` even as term size increases.

## Ineffective Approaches

*   **Aggressive Budget Splitting without Scaling**: **Generation 16** (score 12.06) demonstrated that splitting a small budget (90) too aggressively across recursive calls leads to "stunted" terms. Despite high logic complexity, the average term size dropped to 9.94, significantly lowering the combined score compared to the 16.45 achieved by **Generation 19**.
*   **Unconstrained Redex Injection**: Earlier iterations (e.g., **Generation 13**) showed that injecting redexes without strict budget checks leads to programs exceeding the 100-node limit. Even in **Generation 24** (score 12.25), the introduction of "Type-Informed Shadowing" slightly lowered the validity ratio (0.99) compared to the perfect 1.00 of **Generation 19**, suggesting that excessive complexity in variable management can risk type-safety.
*   **Random Type Generation for Arguments**: Programs that do not prioritize context-inhabited types for `App` arguments struggle to break the 0.35 threshold for `used_vars`. Generating random types often forces the generator to create new literals or nested structures that ignore the available context, as seen in the lower performance of earlier, less context-aware versions.

## Implementation Insights

*   **Variable Shadowing and Unique Naming**: The current best program (**Generation 19**) and **Generation 24** use `f"v{len(ctx)}"` to generate unique variable names. This allows for deep nesting of lambdas where each new binder is tracked, facilitating the "Redex-Chain" strategy and ensuring that `used_vars` increases linearly with term depth.
*   **Function Type Bias**: All high-scoring programs (**Generations 19-23**) employ a 70% bias toward `Fun` types during type generation. This forces the term generator into `Lam` and `App` logic, which is the primary mechanism for achieving the high `unique_subterms_proportions_average` of 0.96.
*   **Weighted Fallbacks for Validity**: **Generation 19** uses specific fallbacks for different types (e.g., `("Int", 42)`) when the budget is exhausted. This ensures that even when complex redex-chain logic fails to find a valid path, the program still returns a valid, type-correct term, maintaining the 1.00 `good_ratio`.
*   **Dynamic Redex Triggering**: The use of a probability-based trigger (e.g., `rng.random() < 0.3` for Redex-Chains) allows the generator to interleave highly complex injected structures with standard type-directed generation, preventing terms from becoming too repetitive.

## Performance Analysis

*   **The 12.43 Benchmark**: **Generation 19** remains the peak performer (12.43), distinguished by its balance of `applied_lambdas` (0.62), `used_vars` (0.41), and `tm_sizes_average` (16.45). While **Generations 21 and 22** achieved similar scores (12.41), they did not surpass the overall balance of the Generation 19 model.
*   **Size vs. Complexity Trade-off**: There is a clear correlation between `tm_sizes_average` and the final score. **Generation 16** (9.94 size, 12.06 score) vs. **Generation 19** (16.45 size, 12.43 score) shows that increasing term volume—provided it is filled with unique subterms and applied lambdas—is the most effective path to higher scores.
*   **Stability of Diversity Metrics**: Across **Generations 19 through 24**, the `unique_subterms_proportions_average` remained extremely stable (0.92 to 0.96). This suggests that the recursive budget-based approach has effectively "solved" structural diversity, leaving `used_vars` and `applied_lambdas` as the primary variables for further optimization.
*   **Redex Density vs. Validity**: **Generation 22** achieved the highest `applied_lambdas` proportion (0.61) among the recent batch, but its slightly lower term size (15.38) compared to **Generation 19** (16.45) resulted in a lower overall score, highlighting that redex density must be paired with overall term volume.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Implement Deep Currying for Multi-Argument Callables**: Extend the "Medium Priority" lookup to search for variables that return the `target_ty` after multiple applications (e.g., to get `C`, look for `A -> B -> C`). This will generate nested `App(App(Var, arg1), arg2)` structures, which increases `tm_sizes_average` and `used_vars_proportions_average` by utilizing existing context variables as arguments for multi-parameter functions.

2. **Introduce Dynamic Redex-Chain Scaling**: Replace the fixed two-binder "Redex-Chain" with a version that scales length (2 to 4 binders) based on the remaining budget (e.g., if `budget > 70`). This will push the `applied_lambdas_proportions_average` beyond the current 0.62 ceiling by creating denser clusters of redexes in a single branch, effectively multiplying the "Power Move" strategy.

3. **Biased Higher-Order Redexes**: In the "Explicit Redex" and "Redex-Chain" blocks, force the `arg_ty` to be a `Fun` type 50% of the time. This encourages the generation of arguments that are themselves lambdas or applications, significantly increasing structural complexity and the `applied_lambdas` metric by ensuring the "argument" side of a redex isn't just a simple variable or literal.

4. **Greedy Budget Reallocation for Operator Nodes**: Adjust the `App` node budget split from `0.7/0.2` to a more aggressive `0.85/0.1` when the budget is high (>50). By starving the argument side (forcing it to use context variables or literals) and flooding the operator side with budget, you ensure the lambda portion of a redex has sufficient depth to contain nested logic, maximizing the density of applied lambdas per total node count.

5. **Context-Aware Type Generation for Redexes**: Instead of using `gen_type(1)` for redex arguments, prioritize types that are already "inhabited" in the current context. By selecting an `arg_ty` that matches a variable already in `ctx`, you guarantee that the argument to the newly injected redex can be a `Var`, which simultaneously maintains high `used_vars_proportions_average` while building the complex `App(Lam(...))` structure.
