# GLOBAL INSIGHTS SCRATCHPAD

The following are global insights about optimization approaches and their effectiveness:

## Successful Algorithmic Patterns

*   **Aggressive Redex-Chain Nesting**: The current best program (**Generation 19**, score 12.43) and **Generation 15** (score 12.42) both utilize a "Redex-Chain" logic that explicitly constructs `App(App(Lam(Lam(...))))` structures. By allocating a large portion of the budget (60%) to the inner body of these chains, these programs consistently achieve `applied_lambdas_proportions_average` above 0.62.
*   **Context-Driven Argument Type Selection**: A major driver for high `used_vars_proportions_average` (reaching 0.41 in **Generation 19**) is the strategy of selecting `arg_ty` from the existing context (75% probability) rather than generating a new random type. This ensures that the argument branch of an `App` node can be satisfied by a `Var` node, simultaneously boosting variable usage and maintaining a 1.00 `good_ratio`.
*   **Tiered Variable Lookup with Callable Priority**: Successful programs (**Generations 17, 18, and 19**) implement a hierarchy for term generation: first, check for direct type matches in the context; second, look for "callables" (functions in the context that return the `target_ty`). This "Medium Priority" logic ensures that complex application structures are grounded in valid context variables, which is essential for maintaining validity at higher term sizes.
*   **Strategic Budget Scaling for Depth**: **Generation 19** improved the average term size to 16.45 (up from 9.94 in **Generation 16**) by using a higher initial budget (110) and more generous budget splitting (e.g., `budget * 0.6` for lambda bodies and `budget * 0.2` for arguments). This prevents the "aggressive curtailing" seen in earlier recursive attempts while staying safely under the 100-node limit.

## Ineffective Approaches

*   **Aggressive Budget Splitting without Scaling**: **Generation 16** (score 12.06) suffered from a low average term size (9.94) despite a high initial budget of 90. The feedback indicates that "aggressive budget-splitting" in recursive calls prematurely truncated branches, leading to simpler terms and lower overall complexity compared to **Generation 19**.
*   **Random Argument Type Generation**: Programs that do not prioritize context-inhabited types for arguments (seen in earlier iterations and less optimized versions of **Generation 18**) struggle to break the 0.35 threshold for `used_vars`. Generating random types for arguments often forces the generator to create new literals or nested structures that don't utilize the available context.
*   **Unconstrained Redex Injection**: As seen in **Generation 13** (previous insights), attempting to inject redexes without strict budget checks leads to "Incorrect" programs that exceed the 100-node limit. Even in successful programs like **Generation 18**, a 99% validity ratio (compared to the 100% in **Generation 19**) suggests that slight imbalances in budget distribution can immediately impact the `good_ratio`.

## Implementation Insights

*   **Variable Shadowing and Unique Naming**: The best program (**Generation 19**) uses `f"v{len(ctx)}"` to generate unique variable names. This allows for deep nesting of lambdas where each new binder is tracked in the context, facilitating the "Redex-Chain" strategy and ensuring that `used_vars` increases linearly with term depth.
*   **Function Type Bias**: All high-scoring programs (**Generations 15-19**) employ a 70% bias toward `Fun` types during type generation. This forces the term generator into `Lam` and `App` logic, which is the primary mechanism for achieving the high `unique_subterms_proportions_average` of 0.96.
*   **Weighted Fallbacks**: **Generation 19** uses specific fallbacks for different types (e.g., `("String", "xyz")` or `("Int", 42)`) when the budget is exhausted. This ensures that even when the complex redex-chain logic fails to find a valid path, the program still returns a valid, type-correct term, maintaining the 1.00 `good_ratio`.
*   **Dynamic Redex Triggering**: The use of a probability-based trigger (e.g., `rng.random() < 0.3` for Redex-Chains) allows the generator to interleave highly complex injected structures with standard type-directed generation, preventing the terms from becoming too repetitive or predictable.

## Performance Analysis

*   **The 12.43 Benchmark**: **Generation 19** currently holds the highest score (12.43), primarily distinguished by its ability to maintain a 0.62 `applied_lambdas` proportion while pushing `used_vars` to 0.41 and `tm_sizes_average` to 16.45. This represents a more balanced optimization than **Generation 15**, which had a similar redex score but smaller terms.
*   **Size vs. Complexity Trade-off**: There is a clear correlation between `tm_sizes_average` and the final score. **Generation 16** (9.94 size, 12.06 score) vs. **Generation 19** (16.45 size, 12.43 score) shows that increasing term volume—provided it is filled with unique subterms and applied lambdas—is the current path to higher scores.
*   **Stability of Diversity Metrics**: Across **Generations 15 through 19**, the `unique_subterms_proportions_average` remained extremely stable (0.95 to 0.96). This suggests that the recursive budget-based approach has effectively "solved" structural diversity, leaving `used_vars` and `applied_lambdas` as the primary variables for optimization.
*   **Validity Consistency**: The transition from **Generation 18** (0.99 validity) to **Generation 19** (1.00 validity) demonstrates that fine-tuning the budget allocation (using `int(budget * 0.6)` instead of arbitrary subtractions) is critical for maintaining perfect type safety in complex generators.

# META RECOMMENDATIONS

The following are actionable recommendations for the next program generations:

1. **Implement Deep Currying for Callable Lookups**: Extend the "Medium Priority" callable lookup to search for variables that return the `target_ty` after multiple applications (e.g., to get `C`, look for `A -> B -> C`). This will generate nested `App(App(Var, arg1), arg2)` structures, which increases `tm_sizes_average` and `used_vars_proportions_average` by utilizing existing context variables as arguments for multi-parameter functions.

2. **Introduce "Redex-Chain" Depth Scaling**: The current "Redex-Chain" is fixed at two binders (`v1`, `v2`). Implement a version that dynamically scales the chain length (2 to 4 binders) based on the remaining budget (e.g., if `budget > 60`). This will push the `applied_lambdas_proportions_average` beyond the current 0.62 ceiling by creating denser clusters of redexes in a single branch.

3. **Type-Directed Variable Shadowing**: Occasionally (10-15% chance) reuse a variable name already present in the context when generating a `Lam`. This "shadowing" forces the generator to create more complex term structures and increases `unique_subterms_proportions_average` by ensuring that identical-looking subtrees are actually distinct due to their binding depth, while also testing the robustness of the context management.

4. **Biased Argument Selection for Redexes**: In the "Explicit Redex" and "Redex-Chain" blocks, instead of generating a random `arg_ty`, force the `arg_ty` to be a `Fun` type 50% of the time. This encourages "Higher-Order Redexes" where the argument being passed into a lambda is itself a lambda or an application, significantly increasing structural complexity and the `applied_lambdas` metric.

5. **Dynamic Budget Reallocation for High-Value Nodes**: Replace the static `budget * 0.7` and `budget * 0.2` splits with a "greedy" allocation that gives 80-85% of the remaining budget to the "operator" side of an `App` if a redex is being injected. This ensures that the lambda portion of the redex has enough budget to be a complex, multi-variable term, while the "argument" side remains a compact, context-using `Var` or literal, maximizing the density of applied lambdas per node.
